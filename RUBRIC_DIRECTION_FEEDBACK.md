# Rubric Direction Feedback for Higher-Level English Learners

## Recommendation

The hybrid recommendation is directionally right, but for higher-level English learners the emphasis should be:

> **Use an ELA-aligned rubric to assess the writing, ELD-informed strategies to support revision, and 6+1 Traits only as an optional source of feedback techniques—not as a second scoring system.**

Assuming the students can already communicate their ideas in English, the product should feel like an **academic writing coach for multilingual learners**, not primarily an ELPAC test-preparation tool.

## What the Original Recommendation Gets Right

- **ELPAC should not be the sole essay rubric.** Its writing rubrics are connected to particular language-proficiency task types. For example, *Justify an Opinion* for grades 6–12 asks for a position and supporting reasons; that does not cover every ELA expectation, such as using textual evidence, developing analysis, or addressing counterclaims. The [official ELPAC writing rubrics](https://www.caaspp-elpac.org/s/docs/tech-report/ELPAC_Writing-Rubrics.pdf) also show that ELPAC considers development and organization—it is not merely a grammar score.
- **Separating ideas from language mechanics is valuable.** A student can have strong reasoning while still needing help with sentence structure or academic phrasing.
- **Limited, actionable feedback fits the app.** The current approach of letting students choose a revision focus is better than presenting scores across seven traits and asking them to fix everything.

## Recommended Adjustments

### 1. Use one assessment framework, not two competing taxonomies

Use grade- and genre-appropriate ELA criteria organized around:

- Purpose and organization
- Evidence and elaboration
- Language choices and conventions

Smarter Balanced is a useful reference for these categories, although its exact rubrics vary by task. Its [technical documentation](https://technicalreports.smarterbalanced.org/2024-25_interim-report/_book/test-design.html) confirms the four-point organization/purpose and evidence/elaboration rubrics plus a two-point conventions rubric. Describe the implementation as **“Smarter Balanced-inspired”** unless it faithfully implements the applicable official rubric.

### 2. Make linguistic scaffolding optional and appropriately challenging

Higher-level learners may find basic sentence frames repetitive or condescending. Offer support when relevant, then fade it:

- “This evidence matters because…”
- “While some readers may argue…, the evidence suggests…”
- “How could you combine these sentences without repeating the same idea?”

Also include sentence combining, cohesion, precise vocabulary, register, and explanation of evidence—not just correcting verb forms. Students should choose whether they need a frame rather than receiving one automatically.

### 3. Prioritize thinking before polishing

Unless errors seriously obscure meaning, address:

1. Strength of the claim or central idea
2. Relevance of evidence and depth of reasoning
3. Organization and connections between ideas
4. High-impact language patterns

This prevents the app from unintentionally teaching students that good writing means merely producing error-free sentences. It also protects their voice.

### 4. Avoid presenting numerical scores initially

Without validation against teacher-scored writing, an AI-generated “3/4” could look much more reliable than it is. Start with rubric-informed feedback such as “Your evidence is relevant, but the explanation does not yet show how it supports your claim.” Add scores only after evaluating consistency with teachers by grade and genre.

## Reclassification Claim to Correct

The statement that CAASPP “determines whether students exit EL status” is too strong. California’s [February 2026 reclassification guidance](https://www.caaspp-elpac.org/training-and-communication/communication/email/02-02-26-elpac) lists four criteria:

1. English-language proficiency assessment
2. Teacher evaluation
3. Parent consultation
4. Basic-skills performance

CAASPP may contribute district-selected basic-skills evidence, but it does not determine reclassification by itself. Reclassification predictions should remain outside the product promise.

## Bottom Line

The strongest direction is **ELA expectations for what to assess, multilingual-learner pedagogy for how to help, and the existing progressive-disclosure design for keeping feedback manageable**. This serves higher-level English learners without lowering the academic ceiling or reducing them to their language errors.
