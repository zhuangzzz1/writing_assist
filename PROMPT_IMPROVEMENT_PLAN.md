## Part 1 — Validation of the Second Agent's Critique

The second agent's critique is largely sound and catches real errors in the original review.
The item-by-item verdicts below inform the priority tiers in Part 2.

### Corrections accepted in full

**Item 4 — `needs_practice` is NOT merely informational.**  
The review overstated this. `needs_feedback` is always overridden by `_normalize()`, but
`needs_practice` is preserved by the router on standard support routes with skill gaps and
directly controls the V2 practice-validation check in `support_agent.py:169`. Calling both
fields "informational hints" was inaccurate. The recommendation is narrowed: only
`needs_feedback` should be documented as coordinator-redundant.

**Item 7 — V2 Support already has the revision/editing hierarchy.**  
`SUPPORT_SYSTEM_PROMPT_V2` already says: "Prioritize ideas, evidence, and organization
before sentence conventions unless errors seriously obscure meaning." This finding was
wrong as applied to V2 Support. It remains valid for `SUPPORT_SYSTEM_PROMPT_V1` (which
says only "ordered by educational importance") and for both coordinator prompts (which
have no equivalent guidance).

**Item 10 — Practice linkage does not break on inconsistent type strings.**  
The claim that inconsistent `issues[].type` strings break practice linkage was wrong.
Practice exercises are linked to learner choices through `priority_index`, not by matching
skill names (`support_agent.py:184`). The recommendation for a controlled vocabulary
remains useful for consistency but the "breakage" rationale is retracted.

**Item 5 — Missing assignment-context rationale guidance is not a defect.**  
Both coordinator prompts already handle absent context explicitly. Retracted.

**Item 3 — Negative instruction count was wrong.**  
The review said "approximately 20" do-not/never occurrences; the actual count is 35 across
all four prompts. Several are essential safety and anti-fabrication constraints that should
not be converted to positives. The recommendation is narrowed to the subset of negatives
that have clearer positive alternatives without weakening safety guards.

### Corrections partially accepted

**Item 1 — Clarification block length.**  
The second agent correctly notes the first ask_clarification bullet is 113 words (not
"approximately 200"), and that the separate reassessment paragraph is 230 words. Together
they are 343 words — still the densest section in any of the four prompts. The structural
concern (unlock conditions buried at the end of a long block) is valid. Over-clarification
is a risk, not a demonstrated final-run failure on its own — but the real evidence (see
Item 15 below) shows the generated clarification questions are compound and multi-option,
which confirms the risk has materialised in a related way.

**Item 6 — Worked-example instruction density.**  
The density concern is valid. The proposed three-rule simplification in the original
review was unsafe: it would have weakened the answer-preservation and scaffolding
constraints that the second agent correctly identifies as important (the V2 incomplete run
still included an answer-revealing explanation). The plan below restructures the block
without weakening those safeguards.

**Item 2 — Few-shot routing examples.**  
The direction is valid; the example was not. "Teacher would publish it → direct_response"
is incorrect because publication-worthiness does not establish that no actionable issues
exist. The plan replaces it with accurate examples.

**Items 8, 9, 12, 16, 17 — Textual gaps without demonstrated failures.**  
These were presented with more confidence than the evidence supports. They are retained as
low-priority enhancements in Part 2 but framed as optional rather than as bugs.

### Findings confirmed by evidence

**Item 15 — Clarification question complexity is a real pattern.**  
This is the most important finding confirmed by the second agent, and the evidence in
`v2_final_release_summary.json` makes it concrete. The three real clarification questions
generated by the model are:

| Words | "or" options | Text |
|---|---|---|
| 21 | 3 | "What would you like help with for this outline—figuring out your thesis, planning how to develop your paragraphs, or something else?" |
| 29 | 3 | "What topic are you thinking about for your essay, and what kind of help would you like first—choosing a topic, figuring out your main idea, or planning your paragraphs?" |
| 35 | 4 | "What is one topic you might enjoy writing about, even a rough idea—like a hobby, a book, or something at school—and would you like help picking a topic or building your main idea from there?" |

All three are compound sentences presenting a menu of options. For a 10–13 year old,
especially a 5th grader with low writing confidence, this structure demands more cognitive
work than a single focused question. This is the clearest evidence-backed finding in the
entire review.

**Item 13 — Grade-level calibration gap is real.**  
"Match the selected grade level" has no operational definition. The original review's
Lexile ranges were uncited, and no 5th-grade vocabulary failure is demonstrated in the
evidence. The gap in the prompt is real; the severity claim was overstated. Retained as
medium priority.

## Part 2 — Improvement Plan

Improvements are grouped into three tiers by evidence strength and expected impact.
Each entry names the target prompt, the exact change, and the rationale.

---

### Tier 1 — Evidence-backed, implement first

#### 1A. Simplify clarification questions to a single focused question

**Target:** `COORDINATOR_SYSTEM_PROMPT_V1`, `COORDINATOR_SYSTEM_PROMPT_V2`  
**Problem:** Real generated questions present menus of 3–4 options in one compound
sentence. That structure makes the question harder to parse and easier to answer
non-committally ("I'm not sure").  
**Change:** Add after the existing clarification instruction:

```
Write one short question that asks for a single piece of information. Do not
present a menu of options in the same question. Aim for one sentence a student
can answer in two to five words. For example, "Are you working on a first draft,
or do you have something written that you want help improving?" is better than
"Would you like help with your thesis, planning paragraphs, finding evidence, or
something else?"
```

**Why this is safe:** It does not remove any routing logic and does not lower the bar for
when to clarify. It only constrains question structure, making answers more likely to be
resolving rather than vague.

---

#### 1B. Move the support-unlock rule to the front of the clarification block (V2)

**Target:** `COORDINATOR_SYSTEM_PROMPT_V2`  
**Problem:** The positive rule — "route to support_agent once intent is clear" — appears
near the end of a 343-word block. The first 113 words establish conditions for
ask_clarification; the unlock conditions follow. This ordering biases toward the negative
cases.  
**Change:** Restructure the ask_clarification bullet so it opens with the positive unlock
anchor:

```
- Once a student has stated a clear intent — such as wanting feedback on a
  draft, turning an outline into paragraphs, or developing a thesis — route to
  support_agent. A complete draft is not required. Choose ask_clarification only
  when the following conditions make support premature: ...
  [existing conditions follow unchanged]
```

**Why this is safe:** No existing logic is removed. Only the presentation order changes.

---

#### 1C. Correct the few-shot routing examples

**Target:** `COORDINATOR_SYSTEM_PROMPT_V2` (as an addition after the JSON schema)  
**Problem:** No routing examples exist; the original review proposed a bad one
("teacher would publish it → direct_response"). Publication-worthiness is not the test
for direct_response — no actionable issues is.  
**Change:** Add a compact example block after the closing schema paragraph:

```
// Routing examples — not part of the output schema:
// One low-severity word-choice issue in an otherwise strong draft → light_touch,
//   1 issue, direct_response only if no revision would improve the reader's experience
// Weak thesis + unexplained class-vote evidence → standard, 2 issues, support_agent
// Outline with no stated goal, no concern field → ask_clarification
// Personal disclosure involving adult concern + writing → teacher_review
```

### Tier 2 — Clear textual gaps, medium priority

#### 2A. Add grade-level vocabulary anchors

**Target:** `SUPPORT_SYSTEM_PROMPT_V1`, `SUPPORT_SYSTEM_PROMPT_V2`  
**Problem:** "Match the selected grade level" has no operational definition. The evidence
does not demonstrate a failure, but the gap is real — the model has no guidance on how
to adjust language between 5th and 7th grade.  
**Change:** Add after the existing grade-level instruction:

```
Adjust language to the selected grade level. For 5th grade, prefer everyday words
over academic ones and name specific locations in the text ("your second sentence",
"the last paragraph"). For 6th grade, introduce academic terms with a brief gloss
("your thesis — the main idea you're defending"). For 7th grade, use standard
writing-class vocabulary (thesis, evidence, claim, analysis) without explanation.
```

---

#### 2B. Add revision/editing hierarchy to V1 Support and both coordinators

**Target:** `SUPPORT_SYSTEM_PROMPT_V1`, `COORDINATOR_SYSTEM_PROMPT_V1`,
`COORDINATOR_SYSTEM_PROMPT_V2`  
**Note:** V2 Support already has this. Do not add to it.  
**Change:**

*V1 Support* — add after "ordered by educational importance":
```
Place idea and structure priorities before sentence-level priorities. Flag
mechanics and grammar only when they seriously impede a reader.
```

*Both coordinators* — add to the support_agent routing bullet:
```
When identifying issues, flag idea and structure problems (thesis, evidence,
organization) before sentence-level problems (word choice, grammar). Include
sentence-level issues only if they seriously impede meaning.
```

---

#### 2C. Constrain `next_step` granularity

**Target:** `SUPPORT_SYSTEM_PROMPT_V1`, `SUPPORT_SYSTEM_PROMPT_V2`  
**Problem:** "One concrete revision action" spans a wide range of specificity.  
**Change:** Add to the next_step instruction:

```
The next step should name a specific sentence, paragraph, or section in the
student's writing and describe one change the student can attempt in a short
sitting. Avoid broad goals ("improve your thesis") in favour of scoped actions
("add a second specific reason to your thesis sentence").
Accommodate outlines by pointing to a specific placeholder or section.
```

---

#### 2D. Tighten `needs_feedback` documentation only

**Target:** `COORDINATOR_SYSTEM_PROMPT_V1`, `COORDINATOR_SYSTEM_PROMPT_V2`  
**Problem:** `needs_feedback` is always overridden by the router. The prompt asks the
coordinator to set it, which costs tokens and creates false expectations about its
influence.  
**Change:** In the JSON schema trailing paragraph, replace:
```
needs_feedback and needs_practice inform the Support Agent; routing_decision
alone controls the normal route, with flag_teacher as the safety override.
```
with:
```
routing_decision alone controls the route. needs_practice is used by the Support
Agent when routing_decision is support_agent; set it based on whether a targeted
exercise would benefit the identified gaps. needs_feedback is informational and
may be adjusted by the router.
```

---

#### 2E. Restructure worked-example constraints in V2 Support (preserving all safeguards)

**Target:** `SUPPORT_SYSTEM_PROMPT_V2`  
**Problem:** The 229-word, 12-sentence worked-example paragraph competes with craft
guidance. The second agent confirms the safeguards must be preserved.  
**Change:** Convert to a numbered list that retains every existing constraint:

```
Worked examples must follow all four rules:
1. Use a clearly different topic from both the student's draft and the practice
   task. Do not name or suggest that topic in the instructions or steps.
2. Open with the exact label "Hypothetical example:" whenever the example
   includes invented observations, survey results, or factual claims.
3. Never present an unnamed study, statistic, or quotation as real evidence
   ("studies show" is not allowed). Invented data must be visibly fictional.
4. Do not supply a sentence the student could copy as the answer to the exercise.
   The learner must perform the skill, not transcribe the example.
```

---

#### 2F. Add a controlled vocabulary for issue types

**Target:** `COORDINATOR_SYSTEM_PROMPT_V1`, `COORDINATOR_SYSTEM_PROMPT_V2`  
**Note:** The original claim that inconsistent types break practice linkage was wrong
(linkage is through `priority_index`). The vocabulary still improves consistency and
is worth adding as a non-breaking enhancement.  
**Change:** Add after the existing issues/skill_gaps instruction:

```
Use these issue types when they fit; use a plain descriptive label when none
fits precisely: thesis, evidence_framing, organization, development, transitions,
word_choice, sentence_variety, conventions, reflection.
```

## Part 4 — Testing Approach

For each Tier 1 change, run the four frozen fixtures (`normal_case`, `incomplete_case`,
`boundary_case_a`, `boundary_case_b`) against the modified prompt and compare:

1. **Item 1A:** Check that clarification questions are single-sentence with one clear
   question mark and no menu of "or" options.
2. **Item 1B:** Check incomplete_case routing — does it still ask clarification on first
   run, and does it route to support after one clear answer?
3. **Item 1C:** Spot-check that direct_response is not triggered when actionable issues
   exist.

For Tier 2 changes, compare grade-level vocabulary in outputs for the same fixture run
at 5th vs 7th grade. Check that `needs_feedback` changes in the schema commentary do not
affect `pytest` (all 61 tests should still pass — the code already overrides the field).

Do not modify `test_cases/` fixtures or change V1 behaviour visible under the `v1` git
tag. Run `pytest` after each staged change.
