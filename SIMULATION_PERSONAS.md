# Student Personas for Feedback Simulation

**Purpose:** Simulate real student behavior to validate the Feedback Quality Improvement Plan's findings, especially Principle 9 (hint layering by confusion type). Each persona maps to a confusion type and tests specific findings.

**Scenario:** `garden_source_accurate` — the community garden argument with a source. Same draft evaluated in Parts 1 and 2 of the plan.

---

## Persona 1: Maya — Conceptual Confusion

**Profile:** 6th grade English learner, two years in US schools. Strong conversational English, weak academic register. Reads at grade level but stumbles on argument terminology.

**What she understands:** She knows her essay is about starting a garden. She knows 62% is a big number. She wrote "some students are interested in fresh food" because that's what she thought the survey showed.

**What she doesn't understand:** She does not have a working model of "claim" as an argument term. When the feedback says "Connect the survey result to your claim," she reads "claim" as "say something strongly," not "the main argument your essay makes." She doesn't know what "connecting evidence to a claim" looks like as a writing move.

**Why she clicks "Show a hint":** She clicked because she didn't understand the revision suggestion. She's hoping the hint will explain what she's supposed to do, not just give her steps.

**Prediction:** The hint jumps to procedure without restating the concept (Finding 11). Maya will fix "some students" to "most students" (the concrete, understandable part) but won't add the warrant connecting to the claim because she never understood that was the goal. Partial success — the surface error gets fixed, the core reasoning gap remains.

**Tests:** Finding 11 (missing Step 0 concept restatement)

---

## Persona 2: Jordan — Procedural Confusion

**Profile:** 6th grade, native English speaker, grade-level reader. Her teacher has covered argument writing — she knows "claim," "evidence," and "reasoning" as concepts.

**What she understands:** She knows the 62% needs to connect to the main argument. She can see that "some students" understates the number. She understands both revision suggestions conceptually.

**What she can't do:** She can't get the connecting sentence started. She knows she needs to explain why 62% matters for starting a garden, but every time she tries to write the sentence, it comes out circular ("The 62% matters because it's important"). She needs a structural anchor.

**Why she clicks "Show a hint":** She understood the suggestion but needs a concrete starting point. The structural starter is exactly what she's looking for.

**Prediction:** For Finding 1, the starter "This evidence matters because ____." fits the causal move — she'll produce a solid revision. For Finding 2, the starter "When ____, ____." does not fit a cause-effect explanation (Finding 7). She'll fill the slots but the sentence won't do the reasoning work the feedback asked for.

**Tests:** Finding 7 (starter shape misalignment)

---

## Persona 3: Sam — Motivational Avoidance

**Profile:** 6th grade, native English speaker, slightly below grade level. Anxious writer — freezes when facing a blank revision. Understands concepts when explained but doubts his own writing.

**What he understands:** He gets what the feedback is asking. He knows he needs to explain how the survey connects to the garden argument. He can even imagine roughly what the sentence should say.

**What blocks him:** He's anxious about getting it wrong. He's already read his draft and the source. He doesn't need to re-encounter the passage — he needs a low-stakes first action that gets his hand moving.

**Why he clicks "Show a hint":** He's looking for permission and a concrete first step. Not because he didn't understand, but because understanding isn't enough to overcome the freeze.

**Prediction:** The hint opens with "Look at your sentence: 'some students'..." — pointing him back at the passage he already read (Finding 12). This is the most abstract step, not the most motivating one. He'll need the "I'm stuck" option to get unstuck, which gives a more concrete first action: "Circle the words 'some students' in your draft, then write one sentence that replaces them..."

**Tests:** Finding 12 (hints front-load abstract steps)

---

## Evidence to Capture Per Persona

1. Screenshot of feedback state with hint expanded
2. Persona's internal reasoning at each decision point (what they understood, what confused them, what they decided)
3. The actual revision text they produce
4. Second-round feedback: did the revision address the issue?
5. Whether the persona needed the "I'm stuck" option and whether it helped
